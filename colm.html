Colm

I’ve always been interested in analog techniques and devices in design, and in the past few years have heavily relied on my scanner to produce my art. I use it for both school and work, and because of its relatively immobile quality I have grown to be attached to working in my room where I have access to everything I need. My workspace has become so personal and tailored to my interests that I find it hard to produce work I truly love and can proudly say is a reflection of myself if I don’t have access to my scanner and printer. To boil it down further, I basically have a small collection of devices (scanner, printer, magazines…) that I’ve become very attached to. I love the way they work with me and with each other and have accepted their faults as unique and even beneficial to my own work. So much so that I refuse to replace them until I absolutely have to - the thought of finding a new object to replace my old object (which I know and love) seems impossible to commit to if I don’t have a proper reason - in this case, that reason is absolute failure of its function. I’m a firm believer that every object has more value than it lets on, and discarding it before fully exploring it is a crime. I think it is such an important ritual to use your objects to its full potential, then mis-use them to their even fuller potential, and in turn be rewarded with experiences and results that are truly a product of yourself.
